Matchvia: Business & System Proposal
Nov 25, 2026 · @navodya
1. Executive summary
Recommendation: build a venue-first booking and management system for futsal and indoor cricket venues, with a team-focused player booking app on top, launched first in the Kaduwela area (Malabe, Battaramulla, Athurugiriya). Do not build a general multi-sport marketplace that competes head-on with Play.Book.
The opportunity. Futsal and indoor cricket are growing in Sri Lanka: in August 2024 the Cabinet approved commercial futsal stadiums on government land, citing the sport's popularity. Most venues still take bookings by phone, WhatsApp and notebook. Sri Lanka had 14.9 million internet users in October 2026, and Facebook reaches about 10.9 million people, so players are reachable online.
The competition. Play.Book is the established player-side marketplace (six sports, a 3.8% player convenience fee, a Seylan Bank partnership from 1 November 2026). Its focus is players, and its app removed "provider features" in January 2023. That leaves room for a product built around what venue owners and team captains need.
The proposed product.
•	For venues: a free-to-start online calendar, walk-in and phone booking entry, peak pricing, a branded booking page and reports.
•	For teams: recurring weekly slots, split payments, find-an-opponent and small leagues.
•	For the business: monthly venue subscriptions first, then online-payment fees, tournaments and Depthwyn web services.

2. Business analysis
The core problem is coordination: venue owners lose revenue to empty slots, double bookings and no-shows, and players waste time calling around to find a free court. A shared, always-correct calendar solves both sides.

3.3 Industry standards and benchmarks
•	Playtomic (Europe) started as club management software and evolved into a marketplace; it now combines club SaaS fees with a commission on each player booking (Stripe case study, Dealroom). This is the model this proposal follows: win venues with software, then add the player network.
•	Expected features across leading apps: real-time slots, instant confirmation, online or pay-at-venue payment, reminders, group bookings and split payments, open matches, and reviews (BookNplay, Playtomic).
•	Common complaints in competitor reviews: slow or buggy apps, payment failures and poor filters. Reliability is a differentiator in itself.
3.4 Market gaps
1.	Venue operations for futsal and indoor cricket: walk-in entry, recurring team slots, staff accounts and revenue reports, built for owners rather than for players.
2.	Team-sport logistics: recurring bookings, captain-led cost splitting and opponent matching.
3.	Suburban and regional coverage: visible marketing and listings concentrate on Colombo.
4.	Simplicity: plain rupee pricing, no tokens, Sinhala screens, WhatsApp-style confirmations.
5.	Venue branding: each venue's own booking page and website.

6. Technical feasibility
The system is technically feasible for one developer within the budget: a Django web app (mobile-first, installable as a PWA) on a managed PostgreSQL database, hosted on low-cost cloud services, can serve the first 200 venues for well under Rs. 30,000 a month. These are recommendations to confirm in the architecture phase, not final decisions.

6.3 Security requirements
•	Phone OTP login; optional PIN or password for venue staff; sessions expire.
•	Role-based access plus venue-scoped queries in every Django view, proven by automated tests, so one venue can never see another's data. Django's built-in CSRF, XSS, SQL-injection and clickjacking protections stay switched on.
•	No card data stored: payments go through the gateway's hosted checkout; webhooks verified with signatures.
•	Rate limiting on OTP and booking endpoints to block abuse.
•	Encrypted backups; audit log for booking changes and refunds.
•	Data protection: the PDPA's core obligations apply from 1 January 2027, and the Act has cross-border transfer provisions with data-localisation implications (DataGuidance). Choose the nearest cloud region and get legal advice on hosting player data outside Sri Lanka before launch.
6.4 Scalability
•	One well-indexed PostgreSQL database handles thousands of venues; Django app servers can be added behind Cloudflare as traffic grows.
•	Design for many venues from day one (multi-tenant): every record belongs to a venue.
•	Cache venue lists and search results; keep the booking path on the database for accuracy.
•	Background jobs for SMS, reminders and releasing unpaid holds.
6.5 Maintenance strategy
•	Automated tests for the booking engine, payments and permissions; deploy only when tests pass.
•	Separate staging and production environments.
•	Error monitoring and uptime alerts to your phone.
•	Daily database backups, with a restore test each month.
•	A monthly release cycle after launch, plus urgent fixes as needed.
•	Keep Django on a supported version and apply security releases promptly; plan the upgrade to Django 6.2 LTS in 2027.

9. Scalability and expansion
The product should be designed from day one as a multi-venue, multi-area commercial platform, even though it launches in one area. Expansion then means signing venues and adding settings, not rebuilding the system.

9.2 Product expansion
1.	More team sports on the same court model: box cricket, turf football, volleyball, basketball.
2.	Venue operating system: memberships, coaching academies, cafe and equipment sales.
3.	Competition layer: leagues, fixtures, results, player stats, sponsorship slots.
4.	Corporate packages: sports days and monthly corporate leagues billed to companies.
5.	White-label: sell the booking system under a large venue chain's or club's own brand.
9.3 Technical scalability
•	Multi-tenant database from the start; every record tied to a venue.
•	Settings per venue (sports, slot lengths, policies, languages) instead of custom code.
•	Upgrade path: free tiers, then paid managed services, then dedicated database and caching.
•	An API layer that can later serve native apps and partner integrations.

10. Risks and mitigation
The biggest risks are commercial, not technical: venues may not adopt or pay, and Play.Book may add the same features. Each is reduced by validating before building and by staying close to venues.
#	Risk	Likelihood	Impact	Mitigation
R1	Venues are happy with phone, WhatsApp or Play.Book and won't switch	Medium	High	Keep the MVP small so changes after venue demos are cheap; demo early; offer a free tier; position as complementary to Play.Book; you load their data for them
R2	Venues use it but won't pay	Medium	High	Show earned value with data; founding-venue price; keep costs low so break-even needs only 3–5 venues
R3	Play.Book adds venue tools or team features	Medium	Medium	Move faster locally; build relationships and support Play.Book can't match; specialise in team sports
R4	Too few venues in the launch area	Medium	Medium	Map venues first; widen the radius to Kotte and Maharagama if needed
R5	Double bookings or outages destroy trust	Low	High	Database-level overlap constraint; concurrency tests; monitoring; owners can always add walk-ins
R6	Payment failures (a known complaint about competitors)	Medium	High	Use an established gateway; keep pay-at-venue as a fallback; test webhooks thoroughly
R7	PDPA non-compliance from 1 January 2027	Medium	Medium	Privacy notice, consent, minimal data, deletion process; legal check on cross-border hosting
R8	Founder burnout, or a co-founder disagreement	High	High	Split roles between the two founders; signed founders' agreement with vesting; strict MVP scope; phased gates
R9	Budget runs out before revenue	Low	Medium	Free tiers; marketing by visits and community; Depthwyn projects as side income
R10	Economic shocks reduce leisure spending	Low	Medium	Off-peak discounts; corporate leagues; low fixed costs
R11	Name or trademark conflict	Low	Medium	Check names, domains and trademarks before branding
